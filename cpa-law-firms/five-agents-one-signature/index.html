How the agents work

Five agents in sequence. One person signs.

Same line, different rules for each system.

  1. 01
    Collector

    Gathers the documents and facts for the task and lists what is missing instead of guessing.

  2. 02
    Calculator

    Applies the written rule in code and keeps the work line by line.

  3. 03
    Checker

    Ties every number and date to the document it came from.

  4. 04
    Risk reviewer

    Marks what a reviewer, a regulator or an opposing party would question.

  5. 05
    Re-checker

    Recomputes independently and only releases the work if both runs agree.

  6. ✍
    You

    A CPA or attorney reviews, corrects or returns the work, and signs.

The basis

Guardrails that come with every system

The professional signs

No agent concludes a value, signs a return, issues an opinion or gives legal or tax advice. WATW is not a CPA firm or a law firm.

Data stays in your accounts

We work inside the firm's own accounts under a signed data processing and confidentiality agreement before any client information is used. Access is revocable.

Tax return information (CPAs)

Under 26 CFR 301.7216-3, a tax return preparer generally needs the taxpayer's written consent before disclosing return information to a third party unless an exception applies, and consent is required before disclosure to a preparer located outside the United States. The firm decides how that applies to its engagement letters.

Written security program

The FTC Safeguards Rule treats firms that prepare tax returns as covered financial institutions that need a written information security program (IRS Office of Professional Responsibility).

Lawyers and generative AI

ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 29, 2024) addresses competence, confidentiality, communication, supervision and fees when lawyers use generative AI tools, and says informed consent is required before inputting client information into some tools. Your firm assesses each tool and engagement.

No fee sharing or referrals

For law firms the system is a software tool licensed for the firm's use. We do not refer clients or share fees.

Primary sources: 26 CFR 301.7216-3 (eCFR) · IRS OPR: How to be a careful WISP(er) · IRS Publication 5708: WISP guide · ABA Formal Opinion 512. Reviewed November 26, 2026.
