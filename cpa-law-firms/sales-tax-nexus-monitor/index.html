How the agents work

Five agents in sequence. One person signs.

Every month the agents rerun the same routine for each client and state.

  1. 01
    Collector

    Imports sales by ship-to state from the client's store, marketplace or accounting export, and separates marketplace-facilitator sales where the data allows.

  2. 02
    Calculator

    Totals sales and transaction counts for each state over the period that state uses, such as the prior or current calendar year or the preceding 12 months.

  3. 03
    Checker

    Ties each state total back to order lines and verifies that the state's counting rules (taxable vs. gross sales, marketplace sales included or not) were applied.

  4. 04
    Risk reviewer

    Flags states close to the threshold, states with both sales and transaction tests, and clients with physical presence that makes thresholds irrelevant.

  5. 05
    Re-checker

    Recomputes each flagged state independently and drops any status it cannot reproduce.

  6. ✍
    You

    The CPA confirms the status, decides on registration and files.

The basis

The rules, in plain English

Where it comes from

In South Dakota v. Wayfair (2018) the Supreme Court overruled earlier decisions that required physical presence, so states can impose collection duties on sellers with an economic presence.

Thresholds differ

California: more than $500,000 in combined sales of tangible personal property for delivery into the state. Texas: more than $500,000 in total revenue in the preceding 12 months. New York: more than $500,000 in gross receipts and more than 100 sales. Colorado: more than $100,000 in taxable sales. Wisconsin: more than $100,000 in gross sales.

How states count

Some states count gross sales including exempt and resale transactions, some count taxable sales only, and some include marketplace sales. The look-back period also varies.

Timing

Registration and collection dates are state-specific. Texas, for example, requires a permit by the first day of the fourth month after a remote seller exceeds the threshold.

Always changing

States amend thresholds. The agent keeps the threshold table with the source link and the date it was last checked, and flags changes through the regulatory monitor.

Physical presence

Inventory, employees or offices in a state can create nexus even below the economic threshold. The agent flags known locations for the CPA.

Questions

Common questions

What is economic nexus?

It is the connection a state treats as enough to require an out-of-state seller to collect sales tax, based on sales into the state rather than physical presence. After South Dakota v. Wayfair (2018), most states with a sales tax use a sales or transaction threshold.

Are the thresholds the same in every state?

No. Examples include $500,000 in California and Texas, more than $500,000 and more than 100 sales in New York, and $100,000 in several others. States also change them, so verify before registering.

Does the agent register clients with states?

No. It reports status. Registration and filing stay with your firm.

Does it handle marketplace sales?

It separates them where the export allows, because states differ on whether marketplace sales count toward the threshold.
