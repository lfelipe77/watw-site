How the agents work

Five agents in sequence. One person signs.

The agents work from the attorney's checklist. They find and cite; the attorney interprets.

  1. 01
    Collector

    Gathers the client's privacy policy, terms, vendor and data processing agreements and any AI use policy that the firm authorizes.

  2. 02
    Calculator

    Maps each document's clauses to the checklist items the attorney defined for each law and state.

  3. 03
    Checker

    Attaches the exact clause and page for every finding. A finding without a citation is dropped.

  4. 04
    Risk reviewer

    Flags missing notices, differences from the checklist, and items tied to dates that are approaching.

  5. 05
    Re-checker

    Re-reads each flagged clause a second way and keeps only findings that reproduce.

  6. ✍
    You

    The supervising attorney reviews the gap list and decides what to advise the client.

The basis

The 2026 to 2027 calendar

Texas, effective Jan. 1, 2026

The Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act (HB 149) takes effect January 1, 2026 (enrolled bill, Texas Legislature).

Illinois, effective Jan. 1, 2026

Public Act 103-0804 amends the Illinois Human Rights Act on the use of artificial intelligence in employment decisions, including notice to employees, effective January 1, 2026. The Department of Human Rights postponed the hearing on its proposed rules on June 2, 2026.

Indiana, Kentucky, Rhode Island: Jan. 1, 2026

Comprehensive consumer privacy laws take effect in these three states on January 1, 2026 (state codes and the Mayer Brown state privacy tracker).

California, Jan. 1, 2026 and Jan. 1, 2027

CCPA regulations on risk assessments, cybersecurity audits and automated decisionmaking technology took effect January 1, 2026. Risk assessment compliance starts January 1, 2026, and ADMT requirements apply to businesses that use ADMT for significant decisions beginning January 1, 2027.

Colorado, effective Jan. 1, 2027

SB 26-189 repeals and re-enacts the 2024 AI Act with new requirements on automated decision-making technology, effective January 1, 2027. The Attorney General adopted implementing rules on November 19, 2026, with final steps pending.

Moving target

Federal efforts to limit state AI laws are being debated and states keep amending their statutes. The regulatory monitor tracks both.

Does the agent give legal advice?

No. It compares documents to the attorney's checklist and cites clauses. The attorney interprets and advises.

How does this fit with ABA Formal Opinion 512?

Opinion 512 (July 29, 2024) discusses lawyers' duties of competence, confidentiality, communication and supervision when using generative AI tools, including informed client consent in some cases.
