The basis

The rules, in plain English

Bonus depreciation

Section 70301 of P.L. 119-21 provides a permanent 100% additional first-year depreciation deduction for qualified property acquired after January 19, 2025. A written binding contract entered before that date means the property is not treated as acquired after it (IRS Notice 2026-11).

Elections

For property placed in service in the first tax year ending after January 19, 2025, taxpayers may elect 40% instead of 100%. Other elections are covered in IRS Notice 2026-11.

Domestic R&E

New Section 174A lets taxpayers deduct domestic research or experimental expenditures in tax years beginning after 2024, or elect to capitalize and amortize them over at least 60 months. Foreign research must still be capitalized. See Rev. Proc. 2025-28 for transition elections.

Section 179

For tax years beginning in 2025 the maximum Section 179 deduction is $2,500,000, reduced dollar for dollar once qualifying property placed in service exceeds $4,000,000. Amounts are indexed after 2025.

Pass-through deduction

The Section 199A deduction of up to 20% of qualified business income is made permanent for tax years beginning after 2025, and includes a minimum deduction for active qualified business income (IRS, QBI deduction page).

States differ

States do not all conform to federal depreciation and expensing rules. The agent lists each client's states so the CPA can check state treatment.

Questions

Common questions

Is bonus depreciation 100% again?

Yes. P.L. 119-21 provides a permanent 100% additional first-year depreciation deduction for qualified property acquired after January 19, 2025. Eligibility depends on facts such as the acquisition date and property type, which the agent collects for your review.

Can domestic research costs be deducted currently?

For tax years beginning after 2024, Section 174A allows a current deduction for domestic research or experimental expenditures, or an election to capitalize and amortize over at least 60 months.

Does the agent make elections for clients?

No. It prepares comparisons. The CPA decides and files.

Does it handle state conformity?

It flags the client's states for your review. State rules vary and change, so the professional confirms them.
